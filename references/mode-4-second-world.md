# Mode 4 — Second World · 第二世界摄影二创海报

## A design experiment, not a sticker template

The image must feel like an original photographic structure was discovered as a physical object or an opening into another world. The design is successful if the viewer feels: **“原来这张照片本来就可以这样理解。”**

## Layout (hard requirements)

- 3:4 vertical poster, divided into **equal-height upper and lower halves (1:1 split)**.
- Upper half: preserve the supplied source photograph as faithfully as possible; preferably embed an untouched source crop. Subject, pose, perspective, spatial relationships, native color and natural light must remain unchanged. Only necessary proportional crop is permitted.
- Lower half: warm creamy-white / ivory paper, substantially blank. Sparse hand-crafted printed composition; minimal black-line sketches. The paper is an active space, not an empty design failure.

## Reasoning before drawing

1. Identify the **singular most explanatory visual structure**, not just topic: water ripples, shoreline, a road, a reflection, light beam, shadow edge, cloud bank, branch, railing, ridge, doorway, stair, hand gesture, or related source-specific geometry.
2. Ask internally: **“If this exact photographed structure could really be touched, borrowed, crossed, pulled, repaired, opened or used, what would it naturally become?”**
3. Choose the clearest *one* scene-specific interpretation, never a repeated metaphor across unrelated photographs.
4. Decide the boundary's shape based on that idea. The shape of the photographic cut/paper edge must help explain the scene. It need not have a generic ragged tear.
5. Arrange the intervention as a coherent physical interaction: a line can continue across the boundary, a road can extend, water may pour into a vessel, a reflected world can have its own entry, a beam might become something held or measured.

## Optional interaction figures

- Add **0 to 3** very simple black-ink hand-sketched figures **only when the scene requires a user/participant**.
- These figures must *physically interact* with the source's structure (pulling the beam, holding a fold, crossing an extended road, fishing a ripple); they cannot merely stand beside the photo.
- If the actual photo already contains an expressive human action, prefer zero figures.
- Figure size, count and body language should be restrained; never create a dense crowd.

## One-line English annotation

- Write **one short hand-lettered English line** grounded in the exact implied action. Voice: natural, slightly amused, like an overheard aside or understated indie-zine caption.
- Avoid motivational quotes, stock slogans, or templates like “Same..., different...”.
- Position according to negative space and directional force, not mechanically bottom-center.
- User-provided text is followed faithfully; omit text if the user explicitly requests no text.

## Style and exclusions

Real photo + warm ivory paper + sparse black-line drawings; tactile independent-magazine collage, restrained bright clarity. No promotional look, detailed illustration, unmotivated leaf/star stickers, random props, repetitive gestures, photo recoloring, photographic re-staging, or “rip + figure” stencil formula.

## Output checks

- Is there precisely one source-driven second-world mechanism?
- Would the lower area still make sense with the *particular* upper photograph, not another random image?
- Does the cut line actively participate in the visual story?
- Is the original photographic half still accurate?
- If figures were added, are they doing real work?
- Does the English sentence describe this specific scene rather than general inspiration?
